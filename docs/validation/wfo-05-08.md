## Cost selection and enforcement

The local harness alternates fresh/verified-reuse command routes and records
first-feedback, main and active-cycle timings. A diagnostic replay against the
real merged proof includes network latency; its owner-local fresh interpreter is
a separate environment. It cannot establish equivalent-platform acceptance,
native billing or fleet savings. A fast command by itself is never proof.

Three alternating diagnostic repetitions produced these medians:

| Owner-local route | First feedback | Main step | Active two-step cycle |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Full suite, then full suite | 8.89 s | 8.05 s | 16.94 s |
| Full suite, then actual native-proof gate replay | 8.26 s | 22.18 s | 30.34 s |

Here proof verification costs more than rerunning the short local suite. This
result is a reason **not to enable reuse indiscriminately**. It does not contradict
the native container case: container/job startup and transport costs differ.
[Measurement data](wfo-05-08-measurements.json) retains every repetition and native
job interval; no saving is inferred from test counts or summed parallel time.

Choose reuse only where collection/revalidation and additional plan/gate cost are
justified. Cheap checks can cost less to rerun. Unsupported installers/platforms,
mutable dependencies and runtime/package acceptance stay fresh. Existing bounded
consumer collectors retain their behavior until explicitly reviewed adoption.
